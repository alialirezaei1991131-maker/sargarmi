100 kilometers above sea level

The Kármán line, the edge of space

Most international bodies treat this as the boundary between Earth's atmosphere and space. The air is so thin that wings can no longer hold anything up; to stay at this height you have to circle the Earth at orbital speed.

  • 1961Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space and completed one full orbit of the Earth.
  • 408 kmThe International Space Station. Someone has lived aboard it continuously since 2000, and it circles the Earth about every 90 minutes.
  • 384,400 kmThe distance to the Moon. At this page's scale, scrolling to the Moon would take about 61 kilometers of screen.

Inspired by Neal Agarwal's The Deep Sea and Space Elevator. All illustrations and text on this page were made from scratch. Altitudes are approximate and measured from sea level or from the base of a structure.
